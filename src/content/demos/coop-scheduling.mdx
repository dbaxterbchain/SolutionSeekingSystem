<User>

<Assistant>
You've landed on the exact confusion the Fairness principle exists to untangle. Fairness means decisions are just, impartial, and considerate of everyone's needs and contributions. It means applying standards consistently *while also taking individual circumstances into account*. Identical treatment is one tool of fairness. It is not the definition of it.

The system distinguishes **equality** (same treatment for everyone) from **equity** (adjusted treatment to meet real needs). Sometimes fairness requires different arrangements to create genuinely equal burden. An identical weekend rotation that costs Dana her custody time and costs a student nothing comparable isn't equal burden at all; it's equal arithmetic.

Which is why my strongest recommendation is this: before anyone touches the calendar, the co-op should agree on what fairness *means* here, explicitly and in writing. A starter list to react to, not adopt:

- **Burden is shared over time**, not necessarily identically in any single month.
- **Constraints are honored transparently**: an accommodation anyone can see and understand the reason for, and could ask for in the same circumstances.
- **No invisible martyrdom**: nobody quietly absorbs the worst shifts for years; hard shifts get tracked and credited.
- **The reasoning behind every decision is stated out loud**, so nothing looks like favoritism.

Here's my question back: under criteria like those, what happens to Ruth's nine years?
</Assistant>

<Assistant>
Here's a structure, built for a democratic group rather than a pair. Four phases, in strict order, because the order is the whole point:

**1. Constraint-sharing round.** Before any schedule exists, go around the room: equal, uninterrupted time for each of the fourteen members. Each person shares their real constraints: which times are impossible, which are costly, which are merely unpreferred, and what makes them so. This is Mutual Understanding, done as a group. No proposals allowed yet, no responses except clarifying questions.

**2. Criteria check.** Read the agreed fairness criteria aloud. Confirm they still hold, or amend them. Everything after this point gets measured against them, which depersonalizes the arguments: "this draft violates burden-over-time" is a very different sentence from "you're being selfish."

**3. Open proposals.** Anyone, not just coordinators, may propose a schedule, alone or in a small group, provided it honors every stated hard constraint and they can explain how it meets the criteria. Proposals compete on the criteria, not on whose voice is loudest.

**4. Pick one to pilot.** The group selects one proposal (and this is crucial) *not* as the permanent schedule. As a six-week experiment. I'll come back to why that changes everything.

One question first: can you see any member for whom the constraint-sharing round itself would be hard?
</Assistant>

<User>

<Assistant>
No, and the process shouldn't require it. The rule worth writing down: **you owe the group your constraint, not your biography.** "I cannot work Tuesday evenings, and it's a firm constraint" is a complete contribution. The criteria ask that constraints be honored transparently, meaning everyone can see *what* the accommodation is, not that anyone surrender their privacy about *why*.

That said, the system's principles of Vulnerability and Bravery point somewhere real here: the more context people freely choose to share, the easier empathy comes, and constraint-sharing rounds tend to soften rooms in ways that surprise people. Invite openness; never demand it.

A few facilitation notes for running these meetings, since the facilitator makes or breaks this:

- **Rotate the facilitator** each cycle. In a co-op, a permanent facilitator quietly becomes a boss.
- **The facilitator enforces phase order**, kindly and firmly. When someone jumps to a proposal during the constraint round, it gets parked, not debated.
- **Park pair conflicts by name.** If Ruth-and-Dana heat flares, the facilitator names it as a separate conversation with its own track (the Guide work we discussed) and returns the room to the process.
- **Timebox everything.** Equal voice means the confident talkers don't get triple time.

## Scheduling process cheat-sheet

**The diagnosis to share with the co-op:** Our scheduling fights come from skipping Mutual Understanding (we argue positions without hearing each other's constraints) and from past compromises that were never written down, so burdens accumulated invisibly.

### Fairness criteria (starter list: amend together, agree before touching the calendar)

- Burden is shared over time, not necessarily identically in any single month.
- Constraints are honored transparently: accommodations are visible, and anyone in the same circumstances could ask for the same.
- No invisible martyrdom: hard shifts are tracked and credited, including retroactively where burden is already known.
- The reasoning behind every scheduling decision is stated out loud.

### Meeting structure (phases in strict order)

1. **Constraint-sharing round.** Equal, uninterrupted time per member. Share constraints, not positions: impossible times, costly times, mere preferences. You owe the group your constraint, not your biography. Clarifying questions only: no proposals, no rebuttals.
2. **Criteria check.** Read the fairness criteria aloud; confirm or amend.
3. **Open proposals.** Any member or small group may propose a schedule that honors all hard constraints, with a stated case for how it meets the criteria. Proposals are judged against the criteria, never against the proposer.
4. **Select a pilot.** The consent question: "Can everyone live with this for six weeks, knowing there is a review?" Not "which schedule wins?"

### Proposal rules

- Every proposal must honor every stated hard constraint.
- Every proposal must explain itself against each fairness criterion.
- Known accumulated burden (for example, years of holiday coverage) counts as an input, and relief for it should be visible in the draft.

### Pilot

- **Length:** six weeks, dates fixed at selection.
- **Status:** the schedule is a revisable solution under test (a hypothesis, not a verdict). We are a living system; agreements are meant to be revisited.
- **Review meeting:** on the calendar from day one.

### Review questions

- Did the burden land the way we predicted?
- Did anyone absorb more than their share without saying so? Ask directly; check for new invisible martyrdom.
- Do the fairness criteria themselves need amending?
- Keep, adjust, or replace. Then set the next review date.

### Facilitation

- Rotate the facilitator every cycle.
- Enforce phase order kindly and firmly; park early proposals without debating them.
- Park pair conflicts by name into their own track. For any pairing with real heat, each person can prepare separately with the Solution Seeking Guide at /practice/guide before or alongside the group process.
- Timebox every phase so equal voice stays equal.

Bring this as a proposal to the co-op, not a ruling. It should survive its own process. Good luck, and come back after the first review; I'd like to hear what six weeks teaches you.
</Assistant>
